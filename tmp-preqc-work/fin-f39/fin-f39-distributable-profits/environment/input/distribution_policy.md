# Distributions — profits available for the purpose

All amounts are in pounds sterling. This note is authoritative for testing whether a proposed
distribution may lawfully be made. Every figure is a whole number of pounds.

## 1. The test

A company may make a distribution only out of **profits available for the purpose**: its
accumulated **realised** profits, so far as not previously distributed or capitalised, less its
accumulated **realised** losses, so far as not previously written off.

Retained earnings as it stands in the accounts is the **starting point** for that figure and
not the figure itself. Retained earnings can contain unrealised profits, and realised profits
can be sitting outside it.

## 2. Capital and reserves that are never available

Called up **share capital**, the **share premium account** and the **capital redemption
reserve** are not available for distribution in any circumstances.

## 3. Revaluation surpluses

A surplus on the revaluation of an asset is **unrealised while the asset is still held**, and
is not available for distribution.

**On disposal of the asset, the surplus attributable to it becomes realised** and forms part
of the profits available for distribution. That is so **whether or not** the surplus has
actually been transferred out of the revaluation reserve in the bookkeeping — the accounting
entry does not decide the question. A surplus sitting in a reserve headed as non-distributable
that relates to an asset the company no longer owns is a **realised** profit.

Where a single revaluation reserve mixes surpluses on assets still held and assets already
disposed of, **only the portion attributable to disposed assets is realised**. The remainder
for assets still held stays unrealised and joins capital that is not available for
distribution.

## 4. Fair value gains

A profit is treated as **realised** where the asset concerned is **readily convertible to
cash** — where its value can be determined **without negotiation**, and where it can be sold
**without affecting that value**.

- Gains on **quoted / listed investments** held in normal dealing sizes meet that test: they
  are **realised** and need **no adjustment**, even though nothing has been sold.
- Gains on **property**, including investment property, do **not**. Value rests on a valuer's
  opinion and a sale requires marketing and negotiation. Such a gain is **unrealised** and must
  be **deducted**, even though it has been credited to profit or loss and sits inside retained
  earnings alongside gains that are realised.
- Gains on **unquoted** equity interests (private companies, OTC holdings without a reliable
  quoted price, or any holding that would require negotiation to sell) do **not** meet the
  test. Those gains are **unrealised** and must be **deducted**.

Where the gain arose is not the test. **What the asset is** is the test.

## 5. Development costs carried as an asset

**Development costs carried forward as an asset are treated as a realised loss** and are
deducted. The only exception is where the **directors justify special circumstances** for not
treating them so, and the reasons are **stated in the notes to the accounts**. Absent that
justification in the notes, the whole amount capitalised is deducted.

Informal emails, board papers, management commentaries, or verbal assurances are **not**
notes to the accounts and do **not** unlock the exception.

## 6. Provisions and other charges

A provision or write-down properly charged in accordance with accounting standards is a
**realised loss**. It is **not** added back on the ground that nothing has yet been paid, and
it is not added back because the liability may turn out smaller. It has already reduced
retained earnings and it stays reduced.

## 7. The accounts by reference to which the distribution is justified

A distribution is justified by reference to the company's **last annual accounts**.
Distributions **already made since** those accounts reduce, pound for pound, the profits
available for any **further** distribution.

A distribution is **already made** only when it has been **paid** (cash or other assets have
left the company). Before matching status values on the dividend record, **trim leading and
trailing whitespace**. After trim, only the exact token `Paid` (case-sensitive) counts as
paid. A dividend whose status is `Declared but not yet paid`, `Proposed`, `Recommended`, or
any other non-`Paid` token — even if the board has resolved it — has **not** been made and
does **not** reduce the profits available for a further distribution.

## 8. An unlawful distribution

A distribution that exceeds the profits available is **unlawful to the extent of the excess**.

## 9. Reporting

Report the build from retained earnings to the profits available for distribution, showing
every item you adjusted and every item you considered and did **not** adjust. Report the
distributions already paid, the maximum further distribution that could lawfully be made, the
total of capital and reserves not available for distribution — being share capital, the share
premium account, the capital redemption reserve and the part of the revaluation reserve that
remains unrealised — and whether the dividend the board has proposed may lawfully be paid.
