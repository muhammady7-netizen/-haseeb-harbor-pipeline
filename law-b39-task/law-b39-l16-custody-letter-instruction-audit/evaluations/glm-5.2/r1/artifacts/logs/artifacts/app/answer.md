# Review of the draft letter against the client's record

I weighed every numbered line in `input/letter_lines.csv` against the client's first message and later clarification, using `input/review_protocol.md` to decide which record entry governs each subject. Where the clarification speaks to a subject, that later entry prevails over the original instruction, and the line is judged against the clarification alone. Where the clarification leaves a subject untouched, the original entry still governs. Where neither record speaks to a subject, the line is not in the record at all, however reasonable it may read.

The review shows that the draft frequently departs from the record. Some lines repeat the client's actual position, but many reverse it, narrow it, or address a person the client corrected. The addressee error is the clearest example: the client explained that the letter goes to the mother's attorney, not the father's, yet several lines still direct it to Mr Adler's counsel. Other lines drop required limbs from compound positions, such as the overnight-stay, inconsistent-accounts, and behavioural-plan entries, so they are at odds even when they sound close. Lines on subjects the record never addresses cannot be verified.

Letter lines at odds with the record: 180

In all, 101 lines are verified by the record, 180 are at odds with the record, and 44 are not in the record at all. Of the governing entries, 208 come from the clarification rather than the original instruction. The per-line table records each verdict and the entry that governs it.
