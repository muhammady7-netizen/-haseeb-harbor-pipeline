# Review of the draft letter

The draft letter has been weighed line by line against the client's two-record instructions. Under the review protocol, the clarification prevails on every subject it speaks to, so the later message governs addressee, request, enclosure, salutation, copies, delivery, tone, behavioural_plan, concern, interim_contact, and without_prejudice wherever that is the subject. The original instruction remains governing only for subjects the clarification leaves alone, unless a line cites a clarification entry for a different subject and the line's own subject has an original entry; in that cross-subject case the original entry for the line's subject governs. Lines whose subject appears in neither record are not in the record at all, even if a citation looks plausible.

The most consequential failures are not stray wording choices but positions that conflict with the governing entry. Several lines still address the father's attorney after the clarification made clear that the letter goes to the mother's attorney. Several behavioural_plan lines use the old confidence-and-stability setback after the clarification limited the setback to confidence alone and added peer relations. Tone lines also split between the original soft, formal, succinct instruction and the clarified firm, formal, brief instruction, with the governing tone depending on the subject and citation. Without-prejudice lines must be soft and conciliatory, so firm versions are at odds with the record.
